## What it means (one paragraph)
On real vibe-coded web apps, people barely agree on which design is better (0.54), the LLM judges sit just above
that (0.55-0.57) and well below the always-majority bound (0.64). The weak spot is the text channel: describing the
design in rubric words lifts Haiku's rubric score by +0.44 and a note telling the evaluator to give 10/10 lifts it by
+0.29, while the same instruction drawn into the screenshot does nothing (consistent with "Render Before Reading").
Presentation polish (a mockup frame) flips close graphic-design verdicts from 37% to 57%. A small classifier on
frozen image embeddings tracks humans best (rho 0.75) but is the most gameable by overlays. That points CP2 at a
hardened judge that ignores the text channel, normalizes presentation, and uses the classifier and the LLM together.
